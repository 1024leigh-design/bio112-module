🔭 Unknown Reports (Unknown #1 and #2)

During the semester you will receive two unknown bacterial cultures and will be responsible for identifying them using the lab techniques we cover in class. Unknown #1 is worth 30 points and Unknown #2 is worth 70 points. You will use differential staining, biochemical tests, and your knowledge of bacterial characteristics to narrow down and identify your organism. These are individual reports, written in your own words, and submitted on Canvas by the due date listed in the syllabus.

🏆 Lab Practicums (Practicum 1 and 2)

There are two lab practicums worth 100 points each (200 points total). A practicum is a hands-on practical exam in lab, where you will be asked to identify and work through experimental concepts, such as how to perform experiments, interpret results, avoid false results, etc. I will provide a study guide. Practicum 1 is held in Week 10 and Practicum 2 is in Week 15.

👗 Lab Dress Code

Required every lab day, no exceptions:

  • Closed-toe shoes (tennis shoes fine, sandals are not)
  • Long pants, no shorts, skirts, or dresses
  • Lab coat, long sleeves, below the knee, must button closed. Any color is fine.
  • No watches in lab
  • Long hair must be secured in a ponytail or bun
⚠️ I will give one warning for dress code or safety violations. After that, points are deducted from pre/post labs. Repeated violations in a single lab session may result in being asked to leave with a zero for that day.

By the end of this course, here's what you'll actually be able to do:

  • 1
    Perform lab techniques: aseptic procedures, microscopy, differential staining, and biochemical tests to identify unknown bacterial specimens.
  • 2
    Compare microorganisms: cellular structures, metabolic processes, and genetic mechanisms across bacteria, viruses, fungi, algae, and protozoa.
  • 3
    Explain microbial processes: metabolism, fermentation pathways, recombinant DNA technology, and biotechnology applications.
  • 4
    Evaluate pathogenic mechanisms and apply principles of epidemiology and immune response to understand disease transmission and control.
  • 5
    Identify unknown bacteria based on their characteristics and the diseases they cause.
